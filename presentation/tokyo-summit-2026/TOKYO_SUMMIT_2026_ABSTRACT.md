## Abstract

Revisiting the Promise of Automated MEP Quantity Takeoff

 One of the original promises of BIM was that quantities and cost estimates could be generated automatically from digital models. However, particularly in MEP projects, this promise has not yet been fully realized because of incomplete attributes, inconsistent modeling practices, and engineering decisions that cannot be derived from geometry alone.
 This presentation introduces a practical framework for automated MEP quantity takeoff developed by the ENMA Working Group, a practitioner-led openBIM research group. We demonstrate how IFC, IDS, and bSDD can be combined with engineering knowledge and AI-assisted interpretation to bridge the gap between BIM data and practical engineering workflows.
 Our approach starts from the reality that IFC models are often incomplete. We show how geometry, properties, quantities, and connectivity can be progressively enriched through IDS-based validation, bSDD semantic definitions, engineering knowledge, and AI-assisted interpretation. The goal is not perfect BIM data, but practical use of imperfect BIM data.
 The presentation is intended for MEP designers, contractors, BIM managers, software developers, and researchers interested in practical openBIM applications.
 Participants will gain practical insights into the complementary roles of IFC, Properties, Quantities, IDS, and bSDD, and learn how engineering expertise and AI can improve data quality, automate quantity takeoff, and support better engineering decisions.
 Beyond quantity takeoff, this work suggests a broader direction for openBIM. We propose that openBIM should evolve from an information exchange framework into a knowledge-enabled engineering platform, where standardized information, semantic definitions, validation rules, engineering expertise, and AI work together to support practical decision-making throughout the building lifecycle.
